
## Artificial Intelligence: A Selection of Thoughts

Artificial intelligence is, depending on how you look at it, a technical programme,
a philosophical provocation, or a civilisational wager. At its narrowest it is the
engineering of systems that perform tasks previously requiring human judgement. At
its broadest it is the attempt to understand intelligence itself--what it is, whether
it can be built, and what follows if it can.

The field is old enough to have a history and young enough to still be surprised by
itself. The theoretical foundations were laid in the 1940s and 50s by figures like
Turing, von Neumann, and Shannon, who asked what machines could in principle compute,
replicate, or learn. The subsequent decades alternated between ambitious programmes and
sobering failures--symbolic AI, expert systems, the neural network revivals--until the
deep learning era of the 2010s produced systems capable enough to reshape public
debate. The 2020s have brought language models and embodied robots into view
simultaneously, which has made the old questions--about understanding, consciousness,
control, and what we actually want from these systems--newly urgent.

The materials collected here do not aim for a survey. They are a selection of current
threads--some technical, some philosophical, some concerned with governance, a few
deliberately speculative. The organising assumption is that understanding AI requires
thinking about it from more than one direction at once.


### Contents

*[appraisal](appraisal/)* - Critical perspectives on the current trajectory of AI,
from the cautionary voices of leading researchers to philosophical analysis, governance
frameworks, and questions about practice and scaling. The broadest section; a place for
scepticism, scrutiny, and considered appraisal.

*[discovery](discovery/)* - The tension between AI as optimisation and AI as
discovery. What mainstream machine learning actually does, where it runs into limits,
and what researchers like Kenneth Stanley have argued about novelty, open-endedness,
and the unreasonable effectiveness of indirect search.

*[embodied](embodied/)* - The argument that intelligence cannot be disembodied: from
Rodney Brooks's subsumption architecture and the cognitive science tradition through to
the current race to build humanoid robots at industrial scale. Includes material on the
autonomous systems dimension.

*[replicate](replicate/)* - Von Neumann's work on self-replicating machines as
a theoretical foundation for thinking about AI self-improvement, evolutionary
architectures, and what it means for a system to reproduce its own complexity.

*[worldmodels](worldmodels/)* - LeCun's critique of current AI paradigms and his
proposed alternative: world models and the JEPA architecture. Includes a working
implementation on MNIST as a concrete illustration of the ideas.
