
## Design Science Research (DSR) Philosophy


### 1. What is Design Science Research?

Design Science Research is a research paradigm focused on the
*creation and evaluation of artefacts* intended to solve identified
problems. Unlike purely descriptive or explanatory research,
DSR treats *design and construction* as legitimate paths to
generating knowledge.

*Artefacts* in DSR may include:
* Models
* Methods
* Algorithms
* Architectures
* Prototypes / Systems
* Frameworks

The central idea is: Knowledge can be produced by designing something
that did not previously exist and rigorously studying its behaviour,
utility, and implications.


### 2. Historical Roots

#### Early Intellectual Foundations

DSR draws heavily from the work of *Herbert A. Simon*, particularly:
* *The Sciences of the Artificial*

Simon distinguished between:
* *Natural sciences* -> Study what *is*
* *Design sciences* -> Study what *ought to be*

He argued that many disciplines (engineering, medicine, management,
computing) are fundamentally concerned with *designing purposeful artefacts*.


#### Development in Information Systems & Computing

Design Science Research was formalised in Information Systems (IS)
through influential contributions such as:
* *Alan R. Hevner et al. (2004)*
* *Salvatore T. March & Smith (1995)*

These works clarified:
* What constitutes an artefact
* How to evaluate artefacts
* How DSR differs from behavioural research


### 3. Purpose of DSR

DSR aims to:
1. *Solve relevant problems*
2. *Produce useful artefacts*
3. *Generate transferable knowledge*

It bridges:
* Academic rigour
* Practical relevance

Typical outputs include:
* Design principles
* Architectural patterns
* Validated methods
* Demonstrated systems


### 4. Why Use DSR?

DSR is appropriate when:
* The goal is *innovation*, not just observation
* Existing theories or solutions are insufficient
* Practical problems require *new constructs*
* You need both *utility and understanding*

#### Key Motivations

* Many real problems cannot be answered by analysis alone
* Building exposes constraints invisible in theory
* Evaluation produces empirical insight
* Artefacts embody hypotheses about "better ways"


### 5. How Does DSR Work?

DSR typically follows an *iterative cycle*.


#### 1. Problem Identification

* Define the problem
* Establish relevance
* Analyse context

Example: Energy inefficiency in conventional computing for lightweight tasks.


#### 2. Define Objectives of a Solution

* Derive design requirements
* Establish constraints
* Define success criteria

Example objectives:
* Reduce energy consumption
* Improve modularity
* Maintain functional adequacy


#### 3. Design & Development

* Construct the artefact
* Apply design principles
* Iterate structure/function

Example artefacts:
* Distributed microcontroller architecture
* Communication protocol
* Prototype platform


#### 4. Demonstration

* Show artefact functioning
* Use realistic scenarios

Example:
* Text processing pipeline
* Document transformation workflow



#### 5. Evaluation

Assess against objectives:
* Quantitative metrics (energy, latency)
* Qualitative criteria (usability, maintainability)

Methods:
* Experiments
* Simulations
* Comparative studies
* Case studies


#### 6. Iteration

* Refine artefact
* Adjust assumptions
* Improve performance


#### 7. Knowledge Contribution

Extract:
* Design rules
* Patterns
* Trade-off insights
* Theoretical implications


### 6. When Should DSR Be Used?

DSR is particularly suitable when..

#### Appropriate Contexts

* Engineering & system design
* Software & architecture research
* Novel methods or algorithms
* Prototyping new technological concepts
* Sustainability-oriented system redesign


#### Less Suitable When

* Only describing phenomena
* Testing established theories without design
* No artefact creation involved


### 7. Core Philosophical Assumptions

1. *Artefacts are legitimate research outputs*
2. *Utility is a valid evaluation dimension*
3. *Design is a knowledge-generating activity*
4. *Iteration is essential*
5. *Rigger and relevance must coexist*


### 8. DSR vs Other Paradigms

| Paradigm                | Primary Focus                     |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Natural Science         | Explain & predict phenomena       |
| Behavioural Research    | Understand human/system behaviour |
| Design Science Research | Create & evaluate artefacts       |
| Engineering Practice    | Build functional solutions        |

DSR sits between:
* Academic research
* Practical engineering

But differs from routine development by requiring:
* Explicit research questions
* Systematic evaluation
* Generalisable contributions


### 9. Typical Outcomes

* Validated prototype/system
* Design principles
* Architectural patterns
* Evaluation frameworks
* Theoretical extensions


### 10. Relevance to the Sustainable Computing Project

DSR is a strong fit because your project:
* Addresses a *real problem* (energy inefficiency)
* Produces an *artefact* (distributed MCU architecture)
* Requires *iteration* (design -> prototype -> evaluate)
* Generates *knowledge* (energy-aware computing principles)
