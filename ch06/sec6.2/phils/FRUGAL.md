
## Minimalism / Frugal Engineering

### 1. What is Minimalism / Frugal Engineering?

*Frugal Engineering* (often linked with Minimalism in design)
is a philosophy that emphasises:
* Delivering essential functionality
* Minimising resource use
* Reducing cost, energy, and material intensity
* Designing for constraints rather than abundance

Core idea: Innovation emerges from doing more with less:
less energy, less material, less complexity, less cost.

Minimalism complements this by advocating:
* Simplicity
* Elimination of the non-essential
* Clarity of purpose

Together, they promote systems that are *lean, efficient, maintainable, and sustainable*.


### 2. Historical Roots

#### Engineering Under Constraints

Frugal principles have long existed in:
* Aerospace engineering (strict weight/energy limits)
* Embedded systems
* Resource-constrained environments
* Low-income market innovation


#### Emergence of "Frugal Innovation"

The term gained prominence through works such as:
* *Frugal Innovation*
  by *Navi Radjou*

This perspective highlighted:
* Constraint-driven creativity
* Affordable, scalable solutions
* Innovation beyond high-tech excess


#### Minimalism in Design & Technology

Minimalism has roots in:
* Industrial design
* Architecture
* Human–computer interaction
* Software engineering (KISS principle)

Key themes:
* "Less but better"
* Reduction of cognitive and technical overload


### 3. Purpose of Frugal Engineering

Frugal Engineering aims to:
1. Maximise *value per resource unit*
2. Enable *accessibility and affordability*
3. Improve *efficiency and sustainability*
4. Reduce *unnecessary complexity*

It reframes success from:
* Maximum performance -> Appropriate performance
* Feature richness -> Functional sufficiency


### 4. Why Use Frugal Engineering?

#### Key Motivations
* Environmental constraints
* Energy limitations
* Cost sensitivity
* Maintainability requirements
* Scalability needs


#### Problems Addressed

* Overengineered systems
* Energy inefficiency
* Excessive feature bloat
* High lifecycle costs
* Technological inequity


### 5. How Does Frugal Engineering Work?

#### 1. Identify Core Value

Ask:
* What problem must be solved?
* What functionality is truly essential?


#### 2. Eliminate the Non-Essential

Remove:
* Redundant features
* Excess precision
* Unnecessary layers
* Overly complex dependencies


#### 3. Design for Constraints

Treat constraints as *drivers*, not obstacles:
* Limited power
* Limited memory
* Limited bandwidth
* Limited materials


#### 4. Optimise Resource Efficiency

Focus on:
* Energy proportionality
* Material minimisation
* Computational efficiency


#### 5. Prefer Simplicity & Robustness

* Fewer components
* Clear interfaces
* Reduced failure points


#### 6. Lifecycle Awareness

Consider:
* Production cost
* Operating cost
* Maintenance
* Repairability
* End-of-life impact


### 6. When Should Frugal Engineering Be Used?

#### Appropriate Contexts

* Sustainability-focused projects
* Resource-constrained systems
* Low-power computing
* Emerging markets
* Embedded / IoT systems
* Educational platforms


#### Less Suitable When

* Extreme performance dominates all constraints
* Safety margins require redundancy-heavy designs
* Abundance of resources is irrelevant


### 7. Core Philosophical Assumptions

1. *Constraints stimulate innovation*
2. *Sufficiency can outperform excess*
3. *Complexity carries hidden costs*
4. *Efficiency is multidimensional (energy, cost, cognition)*
5. *Value ≠ Feature count*


### 8. Minimalism vs Conventional Engineering

| Conventional Approach      | Frugal / Minimalist Approach   |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Maximise features          | Maximise essential value       |
| Assume abundant resources  | Assume scarcity/constraints    |
| Optimise peak performance  | Optimise efficiency & adequacy |
| Add layers for flexibility | Reduce layers for clarity      |
| Complexity tolerated       | Complexity questioned          |


### 9. Typical Outcomes

* Lower energy consumption
* Reduced cost and material use
* Increased robustness
* Easier maintenance/repair
* Greater accessibility


### 10. Relevance to Sustainable Computing

Frugal Engineering is highly aligned with:
* Low-power architectures
* Microcontroller-based systems
* Minimal software stacks
* Modular computing designs


#### Example Implications

*1. Hardware*
* Use MCUs (Microcontroller Units) instead of high-power CPUs
* Reduce component count
* Favor modularity

*2. Software*
* Minimal runtime overhead
* Task-specific programs
* Avoid bloated frameworks

*3. System Design*
* Right-sized performance
* Energy-aware operation
* Explicit functionality boundaries


### 11. Complementarity with Other Philosophies

*With Systems Thinking*
* Frugality avoids local optimisation traps
* Ensures resource reduction does not shift burdens elsewhere

*With Design Science Research*
* Frugal principles guide artifact design
* DSR evaluates effectiveness and trade-offs
